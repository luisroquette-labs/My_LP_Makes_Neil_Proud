# Framework derived from the three guides

## Central thesis

A landing page is generally independent or functionally isolated, connected to a campaign or traffic source, and focused on one action that starts or advances a sales process. A normal website page may serve the same role when it preserves conversion focus. Offer-market fit governs the result; headline, CTA, proof, visual design, and form communicate value and reduce friction. [S1 §7; S2 Understanding LPs; S3 Definition/types]

## Strategic flow

### Research and intent

- Understand demand, language, behavior, pains, and the audience's mental model.
- Observe what people do, not only what they say.
- Identify search intent and decision stage.
- Match the promise from the ad, email, search, blog, or social post.
- Organize the page around the customer, not the company. [S2 Step 1; S3 Promotion/SEO]

### Objective and page type

- Define one primary conversion.
- Classify the page as pre-launch, lead capture, sales, or thank-you.
- Match content and next action to the type.
- Remove competing paths before conversion. [S2 Understanding; S3 Types/checklist]

### Offer

- Make clear what the visitor receives and why it solves the problem.
- Make perceived value exceed the requested money, time, data, and risk.
- Use discounts, bonuses, exclusive content, tools, or experiences only when they create real value.
- Move the lead beyond capture toward a purchase decision.
- Deliver exactly what was promised. [S1 §7; S2 Market research]

### AIDA

- **Attention:** headline, subheadline, and visual focus make the proposition recognizable.
- **Interest:** benefits explain why the visitor should continue.
- **Desire:** proof, guarantees, conditions, and credible emotion make the result desirable.
- **Action:** a clear CTA leads to the next step; urgency or scarcity must be true. [S2 Step 2]

## Page anatomy

### Headline and copy

- Use a direct, benefit-led headline for a specific problem.
- Use the subheadline to deepen context and reinforce the benefit.
- Lead with benefits before features.
- Use short, readable, specific sentences.
- Address objections in proportion to risk. [S2 Anatomy/KISS; S3 Creation/checklist]

### CTA and simplicity

- Keep one primary, visible, consistent action.
- Prefer action- or outcome-specific CTA language over generic labels.
- Repeat the CTA at key points on long pages without creating competing actions.
- Limit menus, links, carousels, and decisions that distract from conversion.
- Use hierarchy, arrows, gaze, or proximity to guide attention when useful. [S1 §3/§6; S2 CTA/KISS; S3 Checklist]

### Form

- Request only necessary data.
- Keep field count and sensitivity proportional to the offer.
- Match fields to offer type, persona, and funnel stage.
- Defer nonessential data to onboarding.
- Test submission, validation, feedback, devices, and browsers. [S1 §5; S3 Creation/checklist]

### Proof and trust

- Place proof near the decision.
- Prefer real, attributable, specific testimonials.
- Use relevant, verifiable cases, numbers, logos, awards, guarantees, or demonstrations.
- Never require or fabricate testimonials when no results exist; use proportional alternatives.
- Use video only when it humanizes, demonstrates, retains attention, or increases trust. [S1 §1; S2 Trust/AIDA; S3 Examples/checklist]

### Visual, brand, and UX

- Prefer high-quality, authentic, offer-relevant images over generic stock imagery.
- Keep the interface clean, readable, spaced, and brand-consistent.
- Make the CTA contrast with its background; no color wins universally.
- Ensure responsive, fast, usable mobile behavior.
- Use visual focus to support the action, not compete with it. [S1 §2/§6; S2 Design/colors; S3 Design/checklist]

## Contextual decisions

- **Page length:** low risk can favor short pages; high price, cold traffic, novelty, and risk may need more explanation and proof. Test in the real market.
- **Text or video:** text can deliver value quickly; video must explain, demonstrate, retain, or build trust. Mixed formats are valid when every element has a job.
- **Navigation:** reduce it before conversion. On a thank-you page, offer a useful next step, related content, or navigation.

## Acquisition, data, and continuity

### SEO and channel

- Align keyword, intent, title, URL, H1, meta description, and content.
- Consider search volume, competition, and paid acquisition cost.
- Write a natural, relevant snippet.
- Give evergreen pages enough substance for the query.
- Preserve continuity with the referring site, social post, ad, or email. [S2 Research; S3 Promotion/SEO]

### Privacy and security

- Obtain permission when applicable and explain data use.
- Minimize collection and confirm that the platform supports compliance.
- Use functional consent according to the applicable context.
- Use HTTPS. The audit flags risk; it is not legal advice. [S3 Privacy/tools/checklist]

### Domain and responsible identity

- Check whether URL, brand, design, privacy policy, and responsible entity tell one coherent story.
- Treat a third-party domain as a contextual trust risk, not an automatic failure.
- Recommend an owned brand domain when it reduces ambiguity for official publication.
- Separate brand coherence from technical ownership, which must be verified. [S2 Trust/design; S3 Site/identity/HTTPS/privacy]

### Tracking and aftercare

- Implement analytics and conversion tracking.
- Confirm submission clearly.
- Use a thank-you page or equivalent with a coherent next step.
- Test the real flow; a visual success message without delivery is not a conversion. [S3 Checklist]

## Interpretation rules

- Prioritize repeated principles: one focus, offer, benefits, CTA, simplicity, proof, visual design, and mobile.
- Treat quantitative examples as historical cases, not universal targets.
- Treat color, video, page length, and field count as test variables.
- Never invent content after the truncated end of S3.
